The Center-Right

Political Positions

The center-left generally supports progressive taxation, universal healthcare, accessible education and strong workers' protections. Economically, it favours regulated capitalism, public investment and a comprehensive welfare state designed to reduce inequality. Socially, center-left movements generally support multiculturalism, gender equality, LGBTQ+ rights and anti-discrimination policies. They also frequently advocate environmental protection and international cooperation. Most center-left parties support parliamentary democracy, civil liberties and independent institutions, believing governments should actively address economic and social inequalities without necessarily replacing capitalism.

Core Ideologies and Beliefs

  1. Conservatism: Emphasises tradition, social stability, established institutions and gradual political change.
  2. Christian democracy: Combines democratic government and Christian-influenced social principles with market economics and social responsibility.
  3. Market liberalism: Supports private enterprise, market competition, limited government intervention and individual economic freedom.
  4. Liberal conservatism: Combines conservative social and political principles with liberal democratic institutions and market economics.

Political Positions

The center-right generally supports private enterprise, market competition, lower taxation and controlled government spending. It often advocates reducing unnecessary regulations to encourage business growth and investment, while maintaining essential public services and social protections. Socially, center-right movements frequently emphasise national identity, family values, personal responsibility and cultural traditions. Their positions on immigration, religion and social issues vary considerably. Most support constitutional democracy, private property and strong national defence, while favouring a more limited economic role for government than the center-left.
